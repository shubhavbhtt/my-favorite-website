Nepal is ranked among the most disaster-prone countries globally, primarily due to its volatile geographical positioning, fragile Himalayan geology, and extreme seasonal weather patterns. The country is situated directly along the active tectonic boundary where the Indian plate subducts beneath the Eurasian plate, resulting in constant seismic pressure. This tectonic activity exposes Nepal to frequent and devastating earthquakes, most notably the 2015 Gorkha earthquake, which claimed nearly 9,000 lives, displaced millions, and destroyed irreplaceable cultural heritage. The steep, fragile terrain of the Mid-Hills and high mountains compounds this geological risk, giving rise to rampant landslides and rockfalls that regularly isolate remote mountain communities.Beyond these seismic threats, Nepal faces severe climate-induced and meteorological hazards. The annual summer monsoon, which spans from June to September, delivers roughly 80 percent of the nation's total annual rainfall. This concentrated downpour routinely triggers massive riverine floods and sudden flash floods. The impact is most severe in the flat, low-lying Terai plains in the south, which serve as the country's agricultural heartland. These floods wash away vital infrastructure, submerge entire villages, and ruin crops. Furthermore, accelerated global warming has introduced a modern peril: the rapid melting of Himalayan glaciers. This melting has filled high-altitude glacial lakes beyond their capacity, significantly elevating the risk of Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs) that can instantly wipe out downstream valleys.The human and economic toll of these natural disasters is heavily intensified by existing socio-economic vulnerabilities. Unplanned and rapid urbanization, especially within the Kathmandu Valley, has led to dense populations living in structures that lack proper seismic resilience. In contrast, rural regions struggle with extreme geographical isolation, making the deployment of robust early warning systems exceptionally difficult and severely hindering emergency rescue and relief operations during a crisis.Because a vast majority of the Nepalese population relies on subsistence farming, cyclical environmental shocks directly threaten national food security and trap rural families in cycles of poverty. To address these systemic risks, the government established the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) to shift focus from reactive emergency relief toward proactive mitigation and community preparedness. Despite these structural improvements, building comprehensive resilience across such an unforgiving, topographically diverse landscape remains a critical national challenge.
